🐾 What Are Pets?

Pets are your personal digital companions. Adopt one, name it, build its stats through activities, gear it up with equipment, and take it into turn-based battles against NPCs or other players. Every pet is unique — defined by its species, type, elements, and the stat path it carves out as it levels up.

There are 103 unique species to choose from. When adopting you pick:

NameCustomCosmetic only — you can also rename your action labels
Basic element pets are a special case — they deal 10% less damage against every other element, but they receive all three key types from Play instead of just one.
📊 Stats & Health

Every pet has six base stats. These are the foundation of everything — combat power, health, and how well activities go.

Computed Combat Stats
Attack = ATT + DEX Defense = DEF + INT

These are the numbers used when damage is calculated. A high DEX pet punches above its ATT, and a high INT pet defends better than its DEF alone suggests.

Max Health Formula
Max HP = ( (ATT+DEF+INT+DEX+HAP+ENE) / 6 + HAP × ENE ) × 10

HAP and ENE dominate HP because they appear in both the average and the multiplicative term. A pet with HAP=30 and ENE=30 gets 900 from the HAP×ENE term alone. Equipment bonuses to any stat also raise HP.

Specializing in HAP + ENE gives a massive HP pool. ATT + DEX gives high attack. DEF + INT gives high defense and parry potential. DEX + ENE + HAP makes you fast in races.
⬆️ Leveling & XP
XP to Level Up
XP needed for level N = 200 × (1.03 ^ (N − 1))

Level 1 needs 200 XP. Level 10 needs ~261. Level 50 needs ~855. Level 100 needs ~3,532. The 3% curve is gradual — early levels are fast, later ones require sustained effort.

Stat Gains Per Level

Every level up grants 5 stat points distributed randomly across all six stats. Each point is rolled independently — you might get 3 ATT and 2 HAP one level, then 5 DEF the next. Over many levels the distribution tends to even out.

⚔️ How Combat Works

Battles are turn-based. Each turn both sides pick an action simultaneously — neither knows what the other chose. Damage resolves after both choices are locked in.

Damage Formula
Raw Attack = (d20 roll × Attack stat) × charge multiplier × type bonus × element bonus Raw Defense = (d20 roll × Defense stat) × defender charge multiplier × type bonus × element bonus

The d20 roll (1–20) means every hit has natural variance — a roll of 1 deals 1× your attack stat, a roll of 20 deals 20×. This keeps battles unpredictable even between evenly matched pets.

The Parry System

Parry is the most important mechanic to understand. When you defend and your defense roll exceeds the attacker's attack roll, the excess is reflected back as damage to the attacker. A high-DEF/INT pet defending against a weak attack doesn't just block — it punishes the attacker.

Parry damage = Defense roll − Attack roll (when defense wins). A pet with DEF=34 and INT=20 has computed Defense of 54. On a d20=15 roll that's 810 defense value — easily parrying most attacks back at the enemy.
Parry only triggers when the defender's roll beats the attacker's roll. If the attacker rolls high enough to overcome your defense, you take the difference as damage instead.
🎮 The Three Actions
⚔️ Attack
Deal damage this turn. Rolls d20 × Attack stat × charge multiplier × bonuses. If the enemy is defending, their defense roll reduces or reflects your damage. If the enemy is charging, you deal 25% extra. No defense roll on your side when attacking.
🛡️ Defend
Roll defense this turn. If the enemy attacks, your defense roll reduces their damage. If your defense roll exceeds their attack roll, the excess bounces back as parry damage. Defending against a charging enemy blocks 0 attack — no parry possible. Charge multiplier boosts your defense roll.
⚡ Charge
Build your charge multiplier by 1 (up to ×5). You deal 0 damage this turn and take 25% extra if attacked. Your charge carries into future turns and multiplies your next attack or defense roll. Charge resets to ×1 after you attack or defend.
Each species has custom names for its actions (e.g. a Snail might "Slime Lunge" instead of "Attack"). These are cosmetic — the mechanics are identical. You can also set your own custom action names via Rename.

Dual Elements

If your pet has two elements, the game calculates the bonus for every attacker/defender element pair and averages them. A Fire/Holy pet attacking a Necro/Plant enemy gets: Fire→Necro (×1.10), Fire→Plant (×1.10), Holy→Necro (×1.10), Holy→Plant (×1.0) — averaged to ×1.075. Dual elements smooth out your matchups rather than doubling your advantage.

Dual elements are strongest when both your elements beat the same enemy element — you get consistent bonuses across all pairs.

There are 24 rooms total — 12 for battles, 12 for casino games. Every room starts empty. Click one and you get a choice:

🏁 Pet Races

Race your pet against AI competitors. Your pet's speed is calculated from three stats with random variance each race — making it a mix of stat investment and luck.

Speed Formula
Speed = (DEX × rand[0.5–1.5]) × (ENE × rand[0.5–1.5]) × (HAP × rand[0.5–1.5]) / 3

Each of the three stats is multiplied by an independent random factor between 0.5 and 1.5 every race. Higher base stats give you a better average speed, but the variance means upsets happen. AI opponents are generated with stats scaled from yours based on difficulty.

AI Bot Behavior

Bots play with basic poker logic — they fold weak hands, call with medium hands, and raise with strong ones. They don't bluff perfectly, so tight-aggressive play tends to beat them over time.

Buy in with enough XP to survive variance. With 2 bots and a 500 XP buy-in, you have 10 big blinds — enough to play a few hands before you're pot-committed.
🎮 Mini-Games

Two quick XP gambles — Coin Flip and Rock Paper Scissors. Both support 7 visual themes and Fun Mode. Mini-Games rooms on The Floor support two players, so you can play against another user in the same room.

🪙 Coin Flip

Pick heads or tails, set your bet, flip. 50/50 odds. Win and you double your bet. Lose and it's gone. Seven themes change the coin art and the heads/tails labels — Raider, Time, Battery, Electric, Business, Sky, Temperature.

✊ Rock Paper Scissors

Pick your move against an AI opponent. Win pays 1:1, tie returns your bet, loss takes it. Three themes — Traditional, Fantasy (knights/dragons/wizards), War (tanks/planes/infantry). The AI picks randomly, so it's pure 50/50 on win/loss (ties excluded).
